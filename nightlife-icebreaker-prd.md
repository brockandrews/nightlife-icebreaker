# Product Requirements Document
## Nightlife Icebreaker — Digital Human Bingo for Events

**Version:** 0.1 (Pilot / MVP)
**Owner:** Brock
**Status:** Draft for development
**Last updated:** August 30, 2026

---

## 1. Overview

### 1.1 Summary
A mobile web application that turns the classic "human bingo" icebreaker into a digital game for nightclubs, mixers, and promoter-run events. Guests scan a QR code at the door, complete a short survey, and receive a personalized bingo card. Squares are completed by physically meeting other attendees whose survey answers match the square's trait. Meetings are verified via a two-sided QR scan handshake, so progress reflects real in-person interaction.

### 1.2 Problem
Event promoters compete on experience and need new ways to drive engagement, dwell time, and repeat attendance. Guests — particularly those arriving in small groups or alone — tend to stay within their existing circle. A paper version of this game, observed in the wild, successfully pushed attendees to approach and introduce themselves to strangers. The paper version does not scale, produces no data, and cannot be monetized.

### 1.3 Opportunity
Gamified icebreaker tools exist (Whova, Jam Bingo, Connect Space) but are built and marketed for corporate conferences, trade shows, and team building. No established player targets nightlife and social events. The founding advantage is distribution: an existing network of nightclub and event promoters who can pilot and adopt the product.

### 1.4 Business model
- **Pilot:** first event is free for the promoter. Learnings feed the next iteration.
- **Primary:** per-event pricing charged to the promoter. Per-event fits nightlife better than a subscription, since most promoters do not run identical recurring events.
- **Secondary (optional, later):** a small guest-side access fee (~$1). See §9 — this must never be tied to a prize awarded by chance.
- **Long-term value driver:** the promoter-facing audience data and opt-in contact list generated by each event.

### 1.5 Who pays vs. who plays
The customer is the **promoter**. The user is the **guest**. The game is the hook that gets guests to participate; the data and engagement reporting are what the promoter renews for. Both experiences must be built, but promoter-perceived value determines revenue.

## 2. Goals & Success Metrics

### 2.1 Product goals
1. Get strangers at an event to introduce themselves and hold a real conversation.
2. Give promoters a measurable, repeatable engagement activation they can sell to venues and sponsors.
3. Capture consented attendee data that promoters can use for future event marketing.

### 2.2 Pilot success metrics (single free event)
| Metric | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Scan-to-start rate | Guests who scan the door QR ÷ estimated attendance | ≥ 40% |
| Survey completion rate | Guests who finish the survey ÷ guests who scan | ≥ 60% |
| Activation rate | Guests with ≥1 verified connection ÷ guests who complete survey | ≥ 50% |
| Median connections per active player | — | ≥ 4 |
| Card completion rate | Players completing at least one full line | ≥ 20% |
| Marketing opt-in rate | Players consenting to future contact ÷ players | ≥ 50% |
| Promoter intent | Would the promoter pay for the next event? | Yes |

**Kill/iterate signal:** if survey completion is under ~30%, or the promoter is indifferent afterward, the concept needs rework before further build.

### 2.3 Non-goals for MVP
- Native iOS/Android apps.
- Dating, matchmaking, or directing one specific guest toward another specific guest.
- In-app chat or messaging between guests.
- Payment processing (pilot is free; guest-side fees come later).
- Multi-venue or multi-day event support.
- Post-event social feed or persistent guest profiles across events.

## 3. Users & Personas

**Guest / Player.** At an event, likely drinking, on a phone with one hand, in a dark and loud room. Low patience for setup. Motivated by fun, competition, and the prize. May be socially hesitant — the app's job is to give them a legitimate reason to approach someone.

**Promoter / Organizer.** Buys and configures the game. Cares about crowd energy, guest volume, sponsor value, and re-marketing reach. Needs setup to take minutes, not hours, and needs a live view during the event plus a report after.

**Venue staff / Door.** Minimal involvement. Displays the QR code, may point guests to it. Should require zero training.

**Admin (internal).** Creates promoter accounts, provisions events, monitors abuse, supports live issues.

## 4. Environmental Constraints

These drive most design decisions and must be treated as requirements, not preferences.

- **Dark.** High-contrast UI, large tap targets, dark theme by default. Screen brightness auto-boost when displaying a scannable code.
- **Loud.** No audio-dependent features. Prompts must be readable and answerable without hearing well.
- **One-handed, distracted use.** Every core action reachable with a thumb. No multi-step flows requiring sustained attention.
- **Guests are drinking.** Minimize typing. Forgiving flows, no punishing timeouts on core actions.
- **Camera struggles in low light.** Every QR interaction must have a manual short-code fallback.
- **Spotty venue connectivity.** The app must tolerate brief network loss and queue actions for retry.
- **Phones stay in pockets.** There must be a compelling in-moment reason to pull the phone out — leaderboard movement, near-completion nudges, promoter-triggered announcements.

## 5. Core Mechanics

### 5.1 Check-in
1. Guest scans the venue's **event QR code** (printed/displayed at the door). This is the only entry point — it gates participation to people physically at the event.
2. QR opens the mobile web app at an event-specific URL. No app download.
3. Guest enters a display name (first name or nickname) and completes the survey.
4. Guest is issued a **player identity**: a unique QR code plus a 4-character human-readable short code.

### 5.2 Survey
- 8–12 questions, target completion under 60 seconds.
- Multiple choice / tap-to-select only. No free text in the core flow.
- Questions map directly to bingo square traits (e.g., travel, hometown, music taste, hobbies, birth month, languages spoken).
- Includes an explicit, separate opt-in checkbox for future marketing contact (must not be pre-checked, must not be required to play).
- Promoter can select from a template question bank and add a limited number of custom questions.

### 5.3 The bingo card
- 5×5 grid with a free center square (configurable to 4×4 for shorter events).
- Each square holds a **trait challenge** ("Find someone who has lived in another country").
- Cards are generated per player from the promoter's active trait pool, and are **not identical between players** — this prevents a solved card from being screenshot and shared as an answer key.
- Card generation must be feasibility-checked against the live attendee pool where possible, so players are not given squares no one at the event can satisfy.

### 5.4 Making a connection (the handshake)
This is the heart of the product and the main anti-fraud control.

1. Player A opens their scanner and scans Player B's code (or types B's 4-character short code).
2. Player B receives an immediate prompt: *"Did you just meet A?"* with Confirm / Dismiss.
3. B must confirm within a **60-second window**. If the window lapses, the attempt expires and no connection is recorded.
4. On confirmation, the connection is recorded **once** for both players, and the system evaluates both players' cards against the other's survey answers.
5. Matching squares are marked complete. Both players see what filled in and why.

**Rationale:** a code shared to a group chat cannot produce a live confirmation from a stranger standing in front of you. Time-boxing the two-sided tap makes remote farming impractical.

### 5.5 Square completion mode (A/B test at pilot)
Two variants, assignable per event or split within an event:

- **Auto-fill:** matching squares complete immediately on handshake. Lowest friction, highest completion rate, but risks guests scanning without actually talking.
- **Prompt-to-reveal:** the handshake reveals a conversation prompt instead of an answer ("This person has traveled somewhere surprising — ask them where"). The square completes after the player taps to confirm they asked. Slower, but produces real conversation.

The pilot should run both, split across the room, and compare observed conversation quality against completion metrics.

### 5.6 Scoring models (promoter selects per event)
- **Most connections by last call (default).** Winner is whoever has the most verified connections — or most squares filled — when the game clock stops. Keeps late arrivals in contention and rewards the target behavior all night.
- **First to complete.** Winner is the first to fill a line (or blackout). Creates a high-energy finish moment the promoter can announce, but disengages players who fall behind.

### 5.7 Tiebreakers
Applied in order:
1. **Time to reach the tied score** — earlier timestamp wins.
2. **Trait diversity** — count of distinct traits collected; rewards meeting a varied crowd over meeting near-identical people.
3. **Earliest check-in time.**

For *first to complete*, the winning square must be locked by a completed mutual handshake; server timestamps resolve ties.

### 5.8 Winner determination and prizes
Prizes are **achievement-based only** — awarded for performance (most connections / first to complete), never by random drawing. See §9.

## 6. Anti-Fraud Requirements

| Rule | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Each unique player pair counts **once**, regardless of how many squares their traits satisfy | Kills the "farm one friend repeatedly" attack |
| Self-scanning blocked | Obvious |
| Participation gated by the event door QR | Prevents pre-farming off-site |
| Mutual confirmation required within 60s | Prevents broadcast/shared-code farming |
| Cooldown between scans (suggested 20–30s) | Makes "harvest 30 people at the door in two minutes" implausible |
| Traits verified from survey data, never self-reported at claim time | Players cannot lie about who they are to fill a square |
| Server-authoritative scoring; client never computes final score | Prevents client tampering |
| All connection events logged with timestamps for post-event audit | Dispute resolution before prize award |
| Rate/anomaly flags surfaced in the admin console | Manual review of implausible leaders before payout |

**Known residual gap:** screenshot-sharing a player QR. The 60-second mutual confirmation makes this awkward enough to be low-risk at pilot scale. Revisit with rotating/expiring codes if abuse is observed.

## 7. Safety & Trust Requirements

Non-negotiable. A single bad incident can cost a venue relationship.

- **No directed matching.** The app issues broadcast trait challenges ("find someone who…"). It must never tell Player A to seek out a specific named individual.
- **No location display.** No in-venue positioning, proximity radar, or "who's near me" features.
- **No profile browsing.** Players cannot browse a roster of attendees or view another player's details before meeting them.
- **Minimal identity exposure.** Only a display name is visible to other players. No last names, no photos in MVP, no contact details exchanged in-app.
- **Block & report.** Any player can block another (blocked pairs cannot connect) and report a guest to event staff from within the app.
- **Dismiss is always available.** Declining a connection request is one tap, unlabeled as negative, and never penalizes either party's score.
- **Not a dating product.** Copy, visual design, and trait questions must stay clearly platonic and event-focused.
- **Age gating.** Events are 18+ or 21+ per venue; check-in must confirm the venue's applicable age requirement.

## 8. Promoter Experience

### 8.1 Event setup
- Create event: name, venue, date, start/end time, game clock start/stop.
- Select survey questions from the template bank; add custom questions (limit for MVP).
- Choose card size, scoring model, and square completion mode.
- Define the prize (display text only — fulfillment is offline).
- Generate and download the event QR code as a print-ready asset (poster, table tent, wristband card).

### 8.2 Live event console
- Real-time counts: check-ins, survey completions, active players, total connections.
- Live leaderboard, projectable to a venue screen (large-format display mode).
- Manual controls: pause game, extend clock, end game and lock scoring.
- Broadcast a message to all players (e.g., "30 minutes left — leaderboard is tight").

### 8.3 Post-event report
- Attendance and funnel metrics (scans → surveys → active players).
- Aggregate demographic and interest breakdown from survey responses.
- Engagement stats: total connections, median connections per player, peak activity time.
- Exportable opt-in contact list (only guests who explicitly consented).
- Final leaderboard and verified winner, with audit trail.

## 9. Legal & Compliance Requirements

**This section is a hard constraint on product design, not a suggestion.**

### 9.1 No paid-entry games of chance
A promotion combining (a) consideration, (b) a prize, and (c) chance constitutes a lottery. In Texas, only qualified nonprofit organizations may conduct raffles under the Charitable Raffle Enabling Act, and cash prizes are prohibited even for them. A for-profit operator charging guests for entry into a random drawing would be conducting an unlicensed lottery — a criminal gambling matter, and an unacceptable risk to a venue's liquor license.

### 9.2 Required design consequence
**The winner must be determined by achievement, not chance.** The prize goes to the player with the most verified connections, or the first to complete their card. This removes the "chance" element entirely and takes the promotion outside gambling law. It is also better game design, since it rewards the behavior the product exists to create.

Any guest-side fee introduced later must **not** be tied to a chance-based prize. If a random-draw mechanic is ever desired, it must be structured as a free-entry sweepstakes with a genuine, visible, equal no-purchase-necessary entry path — and reviewed by counsel first.

### 9.3 Other compliance items
- **Attorney review** of final promotion mechanics and official rules before the first paid event. Rules must be published in-app and state the winner selection method plainly.
- **Privacy:** publish a privacy policy and terms at check-in. Collect the minimum necessary. Marketing consent must be separate, explicit, opt-in, and revocable.
- **Data retention:** define and disclose a retention period for survey and connection data. Provide a deletion path on request.
- **Marketing data ownership:** contractually define what the promoter receives, what the platform retains, and permitted uses. Guests must be told who they are consenting to hear from.
- **Age verification** consistent with venue licensing.

## 10. Technical Requirements

### 10.1 Platform
**Mobile web application. No native app for MVP.** App-store download friction would destroy the door-to-play conversion that the entire product depends on. The QR must open a playable experience in the phone's browser in seconds.

- Responsive, mobile-first, dark-theme-first.
- Must work on current mobile Safari and Chrome. Camera/QR scanning via browser APIs, with the short-code fallback where camera access fails or is denied.
- Optional "add to home screen" prompt; not required for play.
- Suggested stack: Next.js front end with a managed Postgres backend and real-time subscription support for leaderboard/handshake events. Final stack is the dev team's call, but the real-time requirement is not optional.

### 10.2 Real-time requirements
- Connection request → recipient prompt must deliver in under ~2 seconds.
- Leaderboard updates should feel live (sub-5-second propagation is acceptable).
- Handshake confirmations, scoring, and clock state are **server-authoritative**.

### 10.3 Reliability
- Queue and retry connection attempts through brief network interruptions; surface clear pending/failed state to the user.
- Handshake windows evaluated against server time, not device time.
- Idempotent connection writes — a duplicate submission must never create a second connection between the same pair.
- Graceful degradation: if real-time fails, polling fallback keeps the game playable.

### 10.4 Scale target for pilot
Design for a single event of up to ~500 concurrent players, with bursty check-in traffic in the first hour.

### 10.5 Core data model (indicative)
- **Event** — promoter, venue, timing, config (card size, scoring model, completion mode), status, door QR token.
- **Player** — event ID, display name, identity token, short code, check-in timestamp, consent flags.
- **SurveyResponse** — player ID, question ID, answer value.
- **Card** — player ID, generated squares.
- **Square** — card ID, trait ID, state, completed-by-player ID, completion timestamp.
- **ConnectionAttempt** — initiator, target, created timestamp, state (pending / confirmed / expired / dismissed).
- **Connection** — unique player-pair per event, confirmed timestamp.
- **LeaderboardEntry** — derived/materialized: player, connection count, distinct trait count, last-scoring timestamp.

Enforce a uniqueness constraint on the unordered player pair per event.

## 11. Build Scope

### Phase 1 — Pilot MVP (one free event)
Everything required to run a single event end to end:
- Door QR → check-in → survey → card generation
- Player identity code (QR + short code) and scanner with fallback
- Mutual-confirmation handshake with 60s window
- Trait matching and square completion (both completion modes, A/B assignable)
- Live leaderboard + both scoring models
- Tiebreakers and server-authoritative winner determination
- Anti-fraud rules per §6
- Block/report and safety constraints per §7
- Promoter setup screen, live console, post-event report with CSV export
- Consent capture, terms, privacy policy

### Phase 2 — Commercialization
- Promoter self-serve accounts and per-event billing
- Guest-side access fee (with §9.2 constraints respected)
- Sponsor branding on cards and leaderboard
- Richer survey template library and question analytics
- Projectable big-screen leaderboard mode with venue branding

### Phase 3 — Scale
- Multi-event promoter analytics and returning-guest recognition
- Recurring event series and templates
- Native app only if data shows it is warranted
- Venue/sponsor-facing packages

## 12. Open Questions

1. What is the optimal survey length? Ten questions may already be too many at a door with a line behind you.
2. Should the card be visible before the first connection, or revealed after, to reduce pre-game drop-off?
3. Does the 60-second confirmation window survive real-world conditions (dead phone, pocket, bathroom)? Pilot will tell us.
4. How is the prize actually fulfilled and by whom — promoter or venue? Who bears the cost?
5. Should trait diversity be surfaced to players as a visible secondary score, or stay a hidden tiebreaker?
6. Do we need a "solo arrival" mode that biases card traits toward easier early wins to hook hesitant guests?
7. What happens to a guest who arrives 20 minutes before last call — is there a catch-up mechanic, or do we accept it?

## 13. Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Guests won't pull phones out in a club | Fatal to the product | Leaderboard hype, promoter announcements, near-completion nudges; measure at pilot |
| Handshake too fiddly in a dark, loud room | High drop-off mid-game | Short-code fallback, generous timing, pilot observation and redesign |
| Scanning replaces conversation | Undermines core value | Prompt-to-reveal mode; A/B at pilot |
| Legal exposure from prize mechanics | Severe — criminal and licensing risk | Achievement-based prizes only; attorney review before first paid event |
| Safety incident tied to the app | Loss of venue relationships | No directed matching, no location, block/report, platonic framing |
| Promoter sees it as a novelty, not a tool | No recurring revenue | Lead with the data and re-marketing list, not the game |
| Camera permission denial | Blocks core loop | Short-code entry path must be first-class, not an afterthought |
